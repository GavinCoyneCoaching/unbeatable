Frustration vs Fascination

The Fork In The Road

You lose the ball. You miss a shot. You get dropped for the game. You get overlooked for the captain's armband. Small moments, most of them — the kind that happen every single week. Most people assume resilience gets built in the big occasions — the final, the county trial, the big exam. I don't think that's true. I think it's built much earlier, in moments like these, at a fork in the road most people don't even notice they've arrived at.

The fork isn't the event. It's the response. One path out of that fork leads to frustration — quick, emotional, reactive. Why me? That's not fair. Here we go again. The other leads to fascination — it pauses, gets curious, and asks what actually happened and what to do about it. Here's the part worth sitting with: most setbacks don't hurt because they happened. They hurt because of what happens next. A missed shot rarely loses a match on its own — giving up after it might. Every player hits this fork multiple times a session. What matters isn't avoiding it. It's noticing it's there.

This week's challenge

After the next mistake — yours or your kid's — just name it out loud, gently: "That's the fork. Which way are we going?" You don't need an answer straight away. Just naming it is enough to start.
