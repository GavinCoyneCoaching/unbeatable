Beyond the Scoreboard

This Isn't An Excuse Not To Care About Winning

Whenever I introduce the four scoreboards, there's usually someone in the room who assumes we're telling players winning doesn't matter. We're not. I want to be really clear about that. This isn't an anti-winning philosophy. Nobody trains all winter hoping to come second. Winning matters — a lot. The point isn't to care less about the result. It's to stop chasing it directly, because chasing it directly is what costs you the very behaviours that produce it.

Win your own scoreboard first — then the team's — then the result. Here's the order that actually works, and it's one of the things we hold onto most tightly inside Unbeatable: Win your own scoreboard first — your effort, your attitude, your discipline, the things fully inside your control. Then help the team win its scoreboard — the collective habits, the communication, the way you play for each other. Only after those two things does the main scoreboard on the wall get its turn. Most players do it backwards. They stare at the result first and try to will it into existence, and the controllable stuff — the only stuff they can actually influence — gets neglected. Neglect it, and you're setting yourself up for disappointment, because you've skipped the two steps that actually produce a result worth having.

This week's challenge

Before the next match, ask your player to name one thing on their own scoreboard they're going to win today — regardless of the final score. Just one. Something fully in their control.
