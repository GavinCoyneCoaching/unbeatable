Communication

The Run Nobody Called For

A player makes a good run into space. No pass comes. "Sorry, I didn't know you were there." The runner made the run but didn't make the call — so the teammate on the ball genuinely never saw it. Repeat that enough times across a season and it curdles into something worse: "you never pass me" on one side, "I never knew you were there" on the other. Same problem, two different grievances, and neither one is really about ability.

The cure for a silent team isn't volume. It's one more helpful word. A silent team doesn't usually lose because of talent, weather or the referee. It loses because information never reaches the people who need it. Silence creates confusion, and confusion creates mistakes — two defenders marking the same runner, two teammates colliding for the same ball. A simple "Man on." "Keeper!" "Time." solves it instantly. Without it, chaos. Most players already know this. They stay quiet because they're worried about sounding bossy, being wrong, upsetting someone. Understandable — but silence has consequences too, and the lack of communication is sometimes as costly as anything else on the pitch. Don't try to become the loudest person on the pitch. Just become slightly more helpful. One extra call. One extra piece of encouragement. That's it — and it's a matter of personal accountability, a high level of response-ability, as much as it is a skill.

This week's challenge

Make one extra call this weekend that you wouldn't normally bother with — "Man on," "Keeper," "Time," "Go again." Just one more helpful word than usual.
