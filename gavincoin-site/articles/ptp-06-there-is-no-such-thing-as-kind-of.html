Paying the Price

There Is No Such Thing As "Kind Of"

Last time: the reward for filling your cup is a bigger cup. But there's a quiet phrase that stops a lot of players ever finding that out. I hear it from players, parents, coaches — myself included. "Kind of."

Kind of effort gets kind of results. "I was kind of listening." "I kind of tried." It sounds harmless. It isn't. Kind of attention gets kind of improvement. Kind of focus gets kind of confidence. And most of us already know where that road ends up. Go back to the espresso cup and the pint glass. Ability matters — but application matters more than most people realise. I've watched players with every advantage fail to use it, and players on worn-out boots on a rough pitch keep improving, session after session, because they squeeze every drop out of the time they've got.

There's a difference between attending training and training. One player spends sixty minutes at the session. Another spends sixty minutes learning. Same pitch, same drills, same hour on the clock — completely different return, because one of them showed up kind of, and the other showed up fully. Am I paying the price, or paying the cost?

This week's challenge

Before your player's next session, swap the usual question for a sharper one: "What are you here to improve today?" Then hold them — gently — to giving it their full attention. Not kind of. Fully.
