The Stairs

The Top Of One Staircase Is The Bottom Of The Next

Here's something that surprises a lot of players. They chase a goal for months. They finally reach the top step. And after a day or two… the buzz fades, and a strange flat feeling shows up. Now what?

The top of one staircase is simply the bottom of the next. Reaching a goal was never meant to be the end of the climbing. It's the start of a new staircase — a higher one. The player who becomes captain didn't wake up one morning wearing the armband. They became reliable. Then coachable. Then consistent. Then supportive. Then respected. Then trusted. The captaincy was just the top step everyone could see. And the moment they reached it, a new staircase appeared: now lead. That's not discouraging. It's freeing. It means there's no final step where you have to have it all figured out — just the next climb, and the next, each one built on the last. Nobody wins a championship in a final. The final is simply where the trophy is handed over. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

When your player reaches a goal they've worked towards, resist the urge to just say "well done" and move on. Ask instead: "Now that you're here — what's the next staircase?" Let them feel the win first. Then help them see the next one waiting. In the final email: what to do with the problems that turn up on every one of these staircases — because they will.
