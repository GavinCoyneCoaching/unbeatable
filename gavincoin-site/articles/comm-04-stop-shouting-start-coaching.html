Stop Shouting, Start Coaching

If you've coached or parented for more than a few years, you'll recognise this reflex: something goes wrong, and the volume goes up before you've even decided to raise it. A lot of us grew up in the generation of screaming, shouting, tough love, physicality. It was just different then. Here's the honest part: coaches don't do it out of badness. It's more "this is how I was taught, and it worked out pretty well, so I'll repeat the process." Nobody sits down and decides to shout. It's inherited.

The way you were coached isn't an instruction manual for how they need coaching. There's still a place for a bit of tough love and directness — used on purpose, not on reflex. But players today are different, and the world around them is different, and there's no built-in adjustment for that when we're just running on autopilot from our own upbringing. Naming that honestly — without blame, for ourselves or the coaches who raised us the way they did — is the first step to actually choosing our tone instead of defaulting to it.

This week's challenge

Think of one phrase you inherited — from a coach, a parent, your own playing days — that you still reach for on reflex. Does it still land the way it once did? Or are you just repeating the process?
