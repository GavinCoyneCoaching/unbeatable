The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

The question that decides what goes in

Something happens — a message, a rumour about the team, a comment from someone on the sideline, a bad mark in a test — and it swallows the whole evening. Not because it's actually that important. Because it feels urgent in the moment. Twenty-four hours later, most of the time, it barely matters. But by then it's already cost the training session, the homework, the family dinner where nobody was really present.

Not everything that feels urgent deserves space right now. Inside The Jar there's a governing check I come back to constantly — a single question that decides what actually earns space: "Does this deserve space in this jar right now?" Not "is this important in general" — plenty of things are important in general. The question is sharper than that: does this deserve space right now, given what's actually in front of you? A big concern can be completely real and still not belong in today's jar. That's the difference between a jar that fills with whatever shows up loudest, and a jar that fills with what you actually decided belonged there.

This week's challenge

Next time something crowds in and demands attention, stop and ask out loud: "Does this deserve space in my jar right now?" If the honest answer is no — not never, just not right now — set it down and come back to it later.
