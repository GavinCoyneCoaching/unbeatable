Beyond the Scoreboard

Results Can Lie

Here's a phrase I say to players and parents more than almost any other: results can lie. Not because the scoreboard is wrong — the final score is always accurate. But it's not always honest about what actually happened.

The scoreboard tells you what happened. It doesn't always tell you why. A win can be built on the right habits — or it can paper over the wrong ones. A loss can mean everything fell apart — or it can mean a team did everything right and still came up short on the day. The scoreboard on the wall only ever shows you one number, and it's the last thing to change in a performance. Everything that produces it happens earlier: the effort, the communication, the discipline, the response to the first setback. Those are the behaviours underneath the result. In measurement terms, the scoreboard is a lagging indicator — it tells you what already happened. The behaviours are the leading indicators — they tell you what's coming next, before the scoreboard catches up. If you only ever measure the result, you're always reacting to the past. If you measure the behaviours, you can actually shape what happens next.
