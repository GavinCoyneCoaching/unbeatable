Communication

Her Words Said Fine. Her Body Didn't.

I asked my daughter how she was getting on. "Fine." Asked again a bit later. "Fine." A third time. "Fine." Except her body language was saying something completely different the whole time — the shoulders, the eyes, the way she wasn't quite meeting mine. The old-school instinct is to go straight at it: "you're wrong," or "the situation's wrong," or just push past it. But that's not listening. That's talking with extra steps.

Listening means hearing what they're not saying too. The fourth layer of communication is the one people forget: coach or parent to player, and it's not about what you say at all — it's about what you actually hear. Not just the words. The words and the body language. Instead of assuming, the better move is: what actually is it? How are you feeling? What has you feeling that way? — and work back from there. It's also about perspective. What's causing them stress and strain might not do a thing for us. But it does for them, and it needs to be treated with the respect it deserves until they're able to put their own perspective on it.

This week's challenge

Ask someone — a player, your own kid — "how are you feeling?" this week. Then do the hard part: Wait. Watch. Don't fill the silence with your own answer.
