Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

Where Is Your Attention Right Now?

Watch a player closely during a match and you'll see how fast their attention moves. Locked in one moment. Thinking about the referee, the score, the player marking them, the mistake from five minutes ago, the next. Most people call that a confidence problem.

Most "confidence problems" aren't confidence problems. They start with attention. Here's the distinction we build everything on. Stand at a window and attention goes outward — the opposition, the weather, the referee, other people, the "what ifs." Stand at a mirror and it comes back to you — your effort, your decisions, your communication, your response. And performance doesn't happen where your attention isn't. If it's tied up arguing with the referee, it isn't available for the next decision. If it's replaying the last mistake, it isn't available for the next touch. This isn't about ignoring the world. It's about noticing where your attention has gone, and how quickly you bring it home. The one question underneath the whole programme: Where is my attention?

This week's challenge

After the next game, ask instead of the usual post-match questions: "Mirror or window — where was your attention today?"
