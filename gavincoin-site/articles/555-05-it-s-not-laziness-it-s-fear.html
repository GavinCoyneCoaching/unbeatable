5-5-5

It's Not Laziness. It's Fear.

There's a player on almost every team who never puts their hand up for the penalty, never tries the risky pass, never speaks first in the huddle. It's tempting to label that "not bothered" or "doesn't care enough."

It's rarely laziness. It's fear. Fear of getting it wrong. Fear of the embarrassment if it doesn't come off in front of everyone. Fear of the responsibility if it goes badly. That's the hidden enemy underneath 5-5-5 — not laziness, fear. And fear is expensive. It costs a team the extra pass that doesn't get tried, the opinion that doesn't get voiced, the run that doesn't get made — all the small contributions that would have added up to something bigger. Often, the very thing a player is avoiding is the exact thing sitting inside their missing 5%. It's not a coincidence. We avoid what we're afraid of, and we call it a preference.

This week's challenge

Instead of asking "why aren't you trying harder," try: "What are you worried will happen if you try that?" Then just listen. What is fear costing you? — worth asking of yourself too.
