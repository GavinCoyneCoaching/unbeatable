Frustration vs Fascination

Lend Them Your Calm

Your player makes a mistake. They're already gutted, already replaying it in their head. And then the sideline gets loud — a coach loses their temper, a parent reacts. Watch what happens next: the player doesn't calm down. They match the energy around them.

They're not looking for you to join their panic. They're looking for you to lend them your calm. Someone once put it to me in a line about parenting that's never left me — and it applies just as much on the sideline as it does at home. People borrow emotions from each other, especially under pressure. If the coach stays composed after the mistake, and a teammate says "go again," something shifts. The mistake still happened. But now the player is borrowing calm instead of panic. And calm creates options — space to think, instead of just react. The emotional state you bring into a moment tends to become the emotional state of the moment.

This week's challenge

Pick the next mistake — yours, your player's, anyone's — and be the calm one in the room, deliberately: "Who needs to borrow my calm right now?"
