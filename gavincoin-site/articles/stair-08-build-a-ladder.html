The Stairs

Build A Ladder

Sometimes you break a goal down into steps… and one of the steps is still too big. Too daunting. Too far. So the player freezes on it — and the whole climb stalls on a single step.

A step you can't take isn't a wall. It's a step that needs a ladder. When a step is too big to climb in one go, you don't quit the staircase. You lean a ladder against that step and break it into rungs — smaller moves that get you up the one step that stopped you. The goal to "get properly fit" is a step most kids freeze on. But "ten minutes after training, twice this week" is a rung. And rungs get climbed.

The skill isn't forcing the big step. It's building the ladder. That's it. Whenever the next step feels too big, don't push harder — break it smaller. There's always a rung small enough to stand on. What's my next step? (And if it's too big — what's the next rung?)

This week's challenge

Find the one step your player has been avoiding. Then ask: "What are three smaller rungs that get us up that step?" Take the first rung this week.
