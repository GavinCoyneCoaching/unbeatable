Progress Over Problems

One of the easiest traps in sport, coaching and life is becoming an expert on your problems. You know every detail, every frustration, every reason things aren't working. You can replay them endlessly. But understanding a problem and solving one aren't the same thing. The problem explains where you are. Progress determines where you're going. Let's be clear — problems matter. Ignoring them isn't the answer; blind positivity isn't either. The question is: how much attention does a problem deserve? Because attention is a resource, and the more we give something, the more influence it has over us. A player misses a chance and spends the next twenty minutes replaying it — while the match continues without them. The opportunity for progress was in the next action. Their attention stayed in the last one. Eventually we have to move from "what happened?" to "what happens next?" — because progress lives in the next step, not the previous one. It's why I love this whole framework. The Stairs don't ask "why aren't you at the top?" They ask "what's the next step?" So don't ignore the problem. Just stop living inside it. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Next time your player is stuck replaying a mistake, ask one question: "What happened is clear. What happens next — what's that?" Then help them take it.
