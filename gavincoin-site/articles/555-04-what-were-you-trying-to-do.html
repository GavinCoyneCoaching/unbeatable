5-5-5

"What Were You Trying to Do?"

A young rugby player tries something in a match he's never tried before. It doesn't come off. One coach's response: "Don't ever do that again." Another coach, same mistake, different question: "What were you trying to do?" Same error. Two completely different messages sent to that kid about what happens next time he thinks about trying something new. Great coaches develop players. They don't just remove mistakes. The first response teaches a player to avoid risk — to play it safe, to stick to what's already proven, to protect themselves from being told off again. The second response does something different. It treats the attempt as information, not a crime. It keeps the door open for the next attempt. This matters directly for 5-5-5, because finding more ways to contribute means trying things that aren't already proven. If every new attempt gets shut down the moment it doesn't work, players stop offering the extra pass, the new position, the different role — and go back to doing only the one thing they know is safe.

This week's challenge

Next time your player tries something and it doesn't come off: Ask "What were you trying to do?" before you say anything else.
