The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

The three words we use to reset

You've seen it. A player's head goes after a bad decision or a rough five minutes. And it's not just their game that suffers — the whole team's focus seems to go with them. In that moment, nobody needs a tactical lecture. They need something quick enough to actually land in the middle of a match.

Sometimes you don't need a new plan. You need a reset. That's why we use a sideline cue: "Jar, jar, jar." Three words, and everyone who's done the work on this philosophy knows exactly what it means — reset your priorities, right now. Big rocks first. What actually matters in the next thirty seconds, not the mistake from five minutes ago or the ten minutes still to come. It works because it's short enough to shout across a pitch and specific enough to mean something. It doesn't explain the whole philosophy in the moment — it doesn't need to. The explaining happened already, at training, in the calm. The cue just brings it back.

This week's challenge

Agree a reset word or phrase with your player or your team this week — "jar, jar, jar" or your own version. Use it once, in a real moment, and see what happens.
