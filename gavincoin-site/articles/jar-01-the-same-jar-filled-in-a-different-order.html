The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

The same jar, filled in a different order

Picture an empty jar on a table. Beside it, three piles — ping-pong balls, marbles, sand. The task: fit it all in. Most of us would tip the sand in first. It's the easiest, it pours, it looks harmless. Then the marbles. And by the time you get to the ping-pong balls — the big things — there's no room left. They just sit on top, half in, half out. Reverse the order — ping-pong balls first, then marbles, then let the sand fall into the gaps — and everything fits. Same jar. Same space. Same amount of stuff. Just a different order.

Same jar. Different order. Everything changes. Here's where it lands for me. Most of us spend our lives wishing for a bigger jar — more hours, more energy, more time. But nobody gets a bigger jar. We all get the same twenty-four hours. So the question was never whether your jar is full. It's always full. The question is what's filling it — and what went in first. The players who improve fastest aren't always the most talented. They're the ones who know what their ping-pong balls are, and put them in first.

This week's challenge

Sit down with your player and ask: "What are your three ping-pong balls this week — the things that have to go in first?" Then help them actually put those in first, before anything else claims the space.
