5-5-5

Championship Teams Don't Have a Hero. They Have Heroes.

You know the team that looks almost unbeatable — right up until one player is missing. Suddenly the passes aren't as sharp, the talk on the pitch drops off, the confidence goes out of the group. It's easy to look at that and think: we lost our best player, that's why we lost the game. But that's usually not what actually happened.

Championship teams don't have a hero. They have heroes. Most teams don't struggle because they're missing a superstar. They struggle because they've become too dependent on one. If every attack runs through the same player, the opposition doesn't need to stop the whole team — they only need to stop one person. The strongest teams are built differently. Not around one extraordinary player, but by helping ordinary players find more ways to help. Nobody needs to score the winning goal. But everybody can communicate one more time, track back one more run, win one more tackle, offer one more option. That's the idea behind 5-5-5. People hear the name and assume it's about everyone improving by five percent. It isn't. It's a question: how can I contribute more? You might even hear it shouted from the sideline as three words — "five, five, five" — meaning exactly that: give a little more, somewhere, right now.

This week's challenge

Ask your player one question after the next session or game: "What's one thing you added to the team today that had nothing to do with scoring or being the best player on the pitch?" If they can't think of one, that's not a failure — it's just the starting point.
