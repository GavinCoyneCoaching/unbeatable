Frustration vs Fascination

Every Problem Leaves Clues

"I'm not getting enough game time." "The coach doesn't rate me." "I'm useless at this." Familiar lines — maybe you've said one of them, or heard your kid say one. They all share the same shape: a verdict, delivered fast, case closed. Except it isn't closed. Because most problems are more complicated than the first story we tell about them, and — this is the important bit — most problems leave clues. The trouble is frustrated people rarely look for them. They're too busy looking for someone to blame.

Frustrated people want answers. Fascinated people look for evidence. Take "I'm not getting enough game time." Frustration stops at "the coach doesn't like me." Fascination asks: how's my training attendance? What's my effort like in the sessions the coach actually sees? What are the players ahead of me doing that I'm not? Suddenly we're not dealing with a feeling anymore — we're dealing with information. This is detective work on your own problem. Look down, at the evidence right in front of you, before you reach for a verdict.

This week's challenge

Pick one frustration that's been sitting with your player — or with you. Instead of naming the villain, list three clues: "What's the evidence? What am I actually missing?"
