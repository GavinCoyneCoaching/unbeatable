Frustration vs Fascination

Success Leaves Clues Too

There's a player on the team, or maybe a step up, who's already doing the thing your kid is chasing — reading the game earlier, striking the ball cleaner, staying composed under pressure. It's tempting to just admire it from a distance. There's a better use for it.

One kind of clue looks down at the problem. This one looks up at someone who's cracked it. If someone's doing something you want to do, on or off the pitch, ask: what was their path? How did they get from knowing nothing to where they are now — skills, habits, mindset, all of it? And then, the important part: where am I on my version of that journey? That last question matters because this was never about copying their journey exactly. It's about taking your version of their steps. Steal from them, ethically. They tried something and it worked — you can try your version of it too. Or skip the guesswork entirely and go straight to the source: "I'm really struggling with this. You've clearly worked it out — any tips?" Most people are far more willing to answer that question than we expect.

This week's challenge

Name one person — a teammate, an older player, anyone — who's already where your player wants to be in one specific skill. This week, ask them one question about how they got there.
