Frustration vs Fascination

Your Frustration Is A Compass

Most people treat frustration as proof that something's wrong. I've come to see it differently — I think it's usually a sign that something needs attention. That's a small shift with a big effect.

The frustration isn't the problem. It's the clue pointing towards the problem. Treat frustration like an enemy and you avoid it, suppress it, complain about it. Treat it like information and you start learning from it — because it's often pointing directly at the thing that needs work. "I'm frustrated because I keep losing the ball under pressure." Good — that's information, not a verdict. Am I rushing the touch? Not scanning before I receive it? Panicking the moment someone closes me down? The frustration is real, but it isn't the destination. It's the signpost. Frustrated people stop at the feeling. Fascinated people move through it, following it to whatever it's actually pointing at.

This week's challenge

Name the thing that's genuinely frustrating your player right now — not the surface version, the real one. Then ask: "If this frustration is a compass, what's it pointing at?"
