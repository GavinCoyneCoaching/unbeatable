The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

Every yes requires a no

Every decision comes with a cost. Not always financial — a priority cost, a time cost, an attention cost. In other words: every yes requires a no. When you're young it's hard to see. Every opportunity looks exciting, every commitment feels manageable. But every time we say yes to something, we're automatically saying no to something else — even if we don't notice. Say yes to another hour scrolling, and you're saying no to recovery, or sleep, or time with family.

Every yes has a hidden cost — you either pay it deliberately, or accidentally. So many people feel overwhelmed because they treat yes as if it's free. It isn't. The cost is just hidden, and it usually shows up later — the opportunity looks exciting now, the fatigue arrives later. The most valuable question I've learned to ask: "If I say yes to this, what am I saying no to?" It forces clarity. It forces honesty. Because The Jar was never really about time management. It's about priority management — and the most successful people aren't those who say yes to the most. They're the ones willing to say no to the right things.

This week's challenge

Before your next yes this week, ask: "If I say yes to this, what am I saying no to?" Say it anyway if it's worth it. Just say it on purpose.
