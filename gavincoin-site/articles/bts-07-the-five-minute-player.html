Beyond the Scoreboard

The Five-Minute Player

Picture two players walking off after a match. One played seventy minutes. The other played five. Who contributed more? Most people assume it's obvious. It isn't.

Contribution and visibility are not the same thing. As players move up age groups, something changes. The guaranteed starter is suddenly on the bench. The captain becomes the newest name in the squad. And quietly, a thought creeps in: if I'm not starting, I must not be important. That's the hard part — and it's not really about football. It's about identity. I watched one of my own daughters live through exactly this. Limited minutes, then first off the bench, then trusted to fill in, then pushing for a start. None of it happened overnight, and none of it happened by accident. It happened because she focused on the scoreboards she could actually control — effort, attitude, preparation, learning — instead of the ones she couldn't: selection, game time, the coach's decision. That's the same principle from a couple of emails back, just applied to the hardest seat on the sideline: win your own scoreboard first. You can win it fully in five minutes. You can win it from the bench. You can win it in training all week and never see a single minute on Saturday.

Don't focus on becoming the player who gets picked. Focus on becoming the player coaches can't afford to leave out. Those are two different goals. The first one depends on someone else's decision. The second one depends entirely on you.

This week's challenge

Ask your player one question this week, whether they're starting or not: "If you only got five minutes on Saturday, what would you do with them?"
