The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

The size of the worry vs. the size of the response

A player misses a chance in the first five minutes of a game — and gives it the same energy, the same head-drop, the same distraction for the rest of the match. One missed chance. Sixty-five more minutes ruined by it. Or a player spends the whole warm-up worrying about a selection decision that hasn't even been made yet, instead of the session that's actually in front of them.

A large concern can swallow enormous resources — even when it has nothing to do with what's in front of you right now. This is the sharpest, most differentiated part of The Jar for me. It doesn't just ask what deserves your attention. It asks how much of you each thing deserves, right now. Something can be a real, legitimate concern — the selection, the mistake, the argument at home — and still not deserve the amount of energy, emotion and headspace it's taking up in this exact moment. The size of the worry and the size of the response you give it don't have to match. In fact, most of the time, they shouldn't.

This week's challenge

Before your player reacts to something that's rattled them, ask: "How much of you does this actually deserve right now?" Not "should I care" — of course they should. Just: how much, right now, given what's in front of you.
