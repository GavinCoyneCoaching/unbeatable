The Missing Stair

Sometimes the staircase looks fine. The destination is clear, the effort is there, the motivation is there — and yet progress still feels slow. The player feels stuck. When that happens, more often than not, one of the stairs is missing. And before you can climb again, you need to find which one.

You don't need a new staircase. You need to find the missing stair. That's what I use the Five Questions for whenever a player feels stuck: 1. Do you know what to do? You can't climb a staircase you haven't identified. "I want to get better" — better at what? 2. Are you doing it? Knowing and doing are very different. Knowledge doesn't create progress; action does. 3. Are you doing it consistently? Success isn't occasional brilliance. It's repeated effort — again, and again, and again. 4. Are you doing it with the right effort? Turning up isn't enough. There's no such thing as "kind of" improving. 5. Are you being patient? Sometimes you're not doing the wrong thing — you just haven't done the right thing long enough. Run through the five, honestly, and you'll usually find the missing stair somewhere in there. Once you find it, progress becomes possible again. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Pick the goal your player feels most stuck on. Walk through the Five Questions together, honestly, one at a time. "Which of the five is the one we're actually missing?" Fix that one before you worry about anything else.
