Frustration vs Fascination

Curiosity Beats Confidence

"I'll try it once I know I can do it." Sounds sensible. It's also a trap, because it means waiting for something that usually only shows up after you've already tried. If I could give a young player one superpower, it wouldn't be confidence — strange thing to say from someone who works with players all day, but stay with me.

Curiosity doesn't need proof. Just: what happens if I try? Confidence is wonderful when you have it. The problem is it comes and goes — one good game and it rises, one mistake and it's gone. Build your whole approach on it and you'll spend a lot of time waiting to feel ready. Curiosity works differently. It doesn't ask for guarantees. It just wonders. The confident player waits until they're sure they can do it. The curious player wonders if they can learn it — and usually, that's the player who ends up confident later. Not because they chased confidence. Because they chased learning. Curiosity survives mistakes, setbacks, bad days. Confidence, when it shows up, is lovely. But curiosity is usually what gets you there.

This week's challenge

Pick one thing your player has been waiting to feel "ready enough" to try. This week, try it anyway — just to see what happens: "What happens if I try?"
