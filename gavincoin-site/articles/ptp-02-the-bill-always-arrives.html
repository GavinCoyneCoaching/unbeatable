Paying the Price

The Bill Always Arrives

Last time: every choice has a price or a cost. Here's the part that's easy to miss — you don't get to skip the bill. You only get to choose when it lands.

Good habits ask for payment up front. Bad habits offer credit. A player who wants more game time already knows what they want. The real question was never "do you want it?" It's "are you willing to pay for it?" — turning up consistently, taking feedback without getting defensive, doing the unglamorous work on fitness nobody sees. The game time is the reward. The work is the payment. Bad habits work the opposite way. They offer credit — skip the session, coast through the warm-up, blame someone else — and none of it feels like a problem today. That's exactly what credit is. It feels free right now.

But it isn't free. It's just not due yet. The bill always arrives eventually. Both accounts get settled — one on your terms, up front. One on its own terms, later, usually at the worst possible time — in the game, under pressure, when it actually matters. Am I paying the price, or paying the cost?

This week's challenge

Ask your player to name one goal they actually want, then ask the follow-up most people skip: "What's the price on that — and are you paying it yet, or still on credit?"
