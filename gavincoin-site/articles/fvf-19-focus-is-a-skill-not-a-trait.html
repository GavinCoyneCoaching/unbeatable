Frustration vs Fascination

Focus Is A Skill, Not A Trait

"She's just naturally focused." "He's easily distracted." We say things like this as if they're fixed — like some players simply come with focus built in and others don't. It's a comforting story. It's also not quite true. Focus isn't a trait. It's a skill. Nobody expects a kid to strike a perfect ball on day one — we accept that takes reps. But somehow we expect focus to just show up automatically, and when it doesn't, we call ourselves undisciplined instead of just untrained. Focus isn't the ability to never get distracted. Nobody manages that, ever. It's the ability to notice you've drifted and bring your attention back. A player misses a shot and their mind wants to bolt straight to frustration. The focused player isn't the one who never feels that pull — it's the one who notices it and returns to the next play. Getting distracted isn't the failure. Staying distracted is.

This week's challenge

After the next lapse in concentration, skip "did you lose focus" and ask instead: "How fast did you bring it back?"
