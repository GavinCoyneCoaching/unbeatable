The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

It's never the rocks that ruin the jar

The most dangerous thing about sand is that it doesn't look dangerous. Hand someone a jar and ask what will cause the biggest problem, and they'll point to the big rocks. But it's rarely the rocks that ruin the jar. It's the sand. Because sand is sneaky. A little here, a little there. Five minutes scrolling, ten minutes of videos, one more episode, one more game. Individually, none of it seems important. Collectively, it quietly fills every space meant for something else. Just because something grabs your attention doesn't mean it deserves your attention. It's the same conversation I have with players who say "I don't have time" to train, recover, or work on weaknesses. Often the issue isn't a lack of time — it's that the sand got into the jar first. We live surrounded by sand: notifications, updates, opinions, comparison, noise. More information than any generation before us, and somehow less clarity. One question changes everything: is this a rock, or is this sand?

This week's challenge

Next time your player reaches for their phone, or drifts to whatever's easiest, ask (or teach them to ask themselves): "Rock, or sand?" Nothing more than that. Just name it.
