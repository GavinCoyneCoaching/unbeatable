Frustration vs Fascination

Don't Give It Oxygen

Someone says something on the sideline. A decision goes against you. A small, needling thing happens that isn't really about the game at all. And it can sit there, in your head, for the rest of the session if you let it. You don't have to let it. Let it burn out. Don't give it oxygen. A lot of what happens in a day just doesn't need mental space. If I treat every small negative as something to store, remember and replay, what's the cost of that? I don't have to take it on board. Someone says something, or a situation plays out — fine. It's like a match lighting: it burns bright, burns hot, and burns out. Then you move to the next thing. By not giving energy to the things that don't matter, you free yourself up to respond properly to the things that do. The alternative is going from one negative to the next to the next, moaning the whole way through, carrying the whole weight of the world on your shoulders for something that was never worth that much attention.

This week's challenge

Name one small thing this week that's been sitting in your head rent-free. Then ask: "Is this actually worth the oxygen I'm giving it?" Let it burn out. Move to the next thing.
