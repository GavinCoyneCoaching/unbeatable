The Stairs

Climb Forwards. Plan Backwards.

Here's a pattern I've seen for years. A player sets a big goal — then immediately starts guessing at what to do first. Train harder? Practise more? Do… something? And the guessing is exactly why so many goals stall.

Success often has less to do with ambition than architecture. The players who actually get there rarely start at the bottom and hope. They start at the top — the goal — and work backwards. If that's where I want to be in 90 days, where do I need to be at the halfway point, 45 days in? And if that's the halfway mark, what does this week need to look like? And if that's this week… what's the next step, right now? That's the whole method in four words: climb forwards, plan backwards. Here's what it looks like for a team. The goal is promotion. How? Win the most matches. How? A ruthless defence and an efficient attack. How? Turn up to training and train with intensity, integrity and intention. How? Arrive with the right mindset. And how do you do that? You take care of yourself first, and get your ducks in a row before you ever reach the pitch. Six honest questions, and the "impossible" goal of promotion has quietly become: look after yourself tonight so you can train properly tomorrow.

Backwards planning turns a wish into a staircase. You map the steps from the destination down to your feet. Then you stop planning and start climbing — one step at a time, in the order you built. More steps. Less gaps. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Take the 90-day goal and work back one level with your player: "If that's 90 days away, where do you need to be 45 days in?" Then again, and again, until you reach a step they can take this week. If they're already partway through a 90-day goal, that 45-day mark is a good honest checkpoint on its own — worth asking about even mid-climb. Next time: what it means when even a backwards-planned step still feels too big — because sometimes it's not the goal that's off. It's the gap.
