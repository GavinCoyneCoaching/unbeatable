Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

Your Brain Wants To Be Right

A player tells themselves, "I'm going to have a terrible game." Watch what happens next. The brain gets straight to work — remember that poor pass? Last week's mistake? That strong opponent? Evidence piles up, the player steps on hesitant and distracted, they underperform, and the brain nods: "See? I was right."

Your brain builds a case today regardless. Make sure it's one that helps you. Now change one thing. Same nerves, same opponent, different question: What can I control? What's my next job? Suddenly the brain hunts different evidence — the hard training, the improvement, the problems already solved. Same brain. Different instructions. Confidence isn't the absence of doubt. It's where you point your attention once the doubt shows up.

This week's challenge

Before the next game, help your player choose the question they hand their brain: "What are you going to ask yourself today — and what's it going to go looking for?"
