Frustration vs Fascination

Don't Hand Them The Extra Man

The referee calls something you don't agree with. It's tempting to spend the next five minutes arguing it, sulking about it, replaying it. Understandable. Also costly.

Stay frustrated about something that's already happened, and you've handed them an extra man. It's hard enough to win matches when it's fifteen-on-fifteen, or eleven-on-eleven. But the moment you take yourself out of the game — even mentally, even for a few minutes, because you're busy being annoyed about something that's already done — you've made it sixteen-on- fifteen. You've handed the opposition a numerical advantage, and they didn't even have to do anything to earn it. Same goes for the referee themselves. You can't control how they referee. "Ah ref, let us play" has never once changed a decision. What works instead is: the referee is going to referee how they want, so how do we adjust our style accordingly? Same with the team that keeps coming down one side — what do we need to do differently? Switch approach, switch players, do something different, right now.

This week's challenge

Next time a call or a moment goes against your player mid-match, skip the argument and ask the only question that actually helps: "What do we do now?"
