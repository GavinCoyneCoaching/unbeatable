Communication

Who Do You Think You Are?

Watch a teenage dressing room closely and you'll see it: a player who should say something to a teammate — about effort, about a missed run, about anything — and doesn't. Because in their head, calling a teammate out risks the response, "who do you think you are, telling me what to do? You're not better than me." Player-to-player communication is its own layer, and it's genuinely tricky in the teenage years, wrapped up in all the social stuff that comes with that age.

Lead well enough, and you earn the right to help. There is a way through it — and it isn't about becoming braver or louder. If a player leads by example, performs at a high standard themselves, that earns them a bit of wiggle room to hold others accountable. And it doesn't have to be a call-out at all. It can be: "What can I do to help you do that better? What do you need from me that'd make it easier?" Go in with a helping hand, not a telling-off. The message gets through, the relationship stays intact, and nobody has to win an argument about who's "allowed" to say something.

This week's challenge

This week, if a teammate needs to hear something from you, try asking instead of telling: "What do you need from me to make that easier?"
