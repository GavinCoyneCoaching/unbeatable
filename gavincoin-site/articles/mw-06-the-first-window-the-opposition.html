Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

The First Window: The Opposition

Before the whistle's even blown, most players have already played the game in their head. "She's quicker than me. He scored three against us last year. They're stronger. They're more experienced." The game hasn't started, and it's already half-lost.

The mistake isn't noticing the opposition. It's staying there. Here's the thing — those observations might be completely true. Sometimes the player they're marking really is quicker. Noticing that isn't the mistake. Staying there is. Good players look. They scan, gather information, prepare — then come back. Others stare, spending the warm-up thinking about who they're marking and the first five minutes proving why they can't compete. The best competitors I've coached don't ignore the opposition. They respect them, prepare for them — and once the whistle goes, they swap "what are they capable of?" for "what am I going to do next?"

This week's challenge

Before the next big game, help your player take the glance — then come home. One specific job for kick-off: "What's your first job, regardless of who's across from you?"
