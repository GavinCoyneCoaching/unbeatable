Communication

The Message And The Meaning

A coach says to a player, "I know you're capable of more." The message, in the coach's head, is simple: I believe in you. But depending on the player, the meaning received can be something else entirely: I'm not good enough. Same sentence. Different meaning. And here's the thing — the problem is almost never intent. Most coaches and parents genuinely care. The problem is translation.

Communication isn't measured by what we say — it's measured by what people understand. Every player hears your words through their own confidence, their own insecurities, their own story. Two players can hear the exact same piece of feedback and walk away with two completely different messages in their head — one lifted, one deflated. A coach I worked with years ago put it as well as I've ever heard it put: "The message is only successful if it lands." So great communicators spend less energy on what they want to say, and more on what the other person actually needs to hear.

This week's challenge

Pick a phrase you use often as a coach or parent. Ask yourself honestly: What am I trying to communicate — and what might they actually be hearing?
