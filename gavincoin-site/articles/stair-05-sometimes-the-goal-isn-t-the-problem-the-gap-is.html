The Stairs

Sometimes The Goal Isn't The Problem. The Gap Is.

The most common thing I hear from players is "I don't think I can do it." The goal changes — county panel, starting team, confidence, a championship — but the feeling stays the same. Too big. Too far. Too overwhelming. Here's what I've noticed over the years: most of the time, the goal isn't the problem. The gap is.

If the climb feels impossible, it doesn't mean the goal is wrong. It might just mean the staircase needs more steps. You're at the bottom of a staircase, the right goal at the top. But the next step feels enormous — not because the destination is unrealistic, but because the gap between where you are and where you're going is simply too large. A player who wants the county team but struggles with attendance, fitness and consistency isn't chasing an impossible goal. They've just tried to leap ten steps at once. Inside Unbeatable we don't lower standards or tell players to dream smaller. We build better staircases. "I want to be confident" becomes: call for the ball once, then three times, then communicate through a drill, then a match. Suddenly confidence becomes trainable.

Don't shrink the goal. Widen the staircase. That's the shift. Before you assume a goal is too big, check whether it actually is — or whether there's just a step missing somewhere in the middle. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Find the goal your player has quietly written off as "probably not for me." Ask: "Is the goal wrong — or is there a step missing between here and there?" Nine times out of ten, it's the second one.
