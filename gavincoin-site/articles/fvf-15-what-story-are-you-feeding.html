Frustration vs Fascination

What Story Are You Feeding?

Here's a question worth sitting with: not what story are you telling — what story are you feeding? Because a story doesn't get powerful the first time you say it. It gets powerful when you reinforce it, over and over, with your attention.

Stories don't get powerful when you say them. They get powerful when you feed them. A player tells themselves "I'm not confident." At first, it's just a thought. Then they start collecting evidence for it — every hesitation, every mistake, every setback becomes proof. The story grows. A player of the same age and ability tells themselves "I'm learning." Now their attention goes hunting for different evidence — every small improvement becomes proof of that story instead. Same reality, wildly different story, because of what each of them chose to feed. That's why focus matters so much here. It isn't just what you look at — it's what you strengthen by looking at it. And the good news is you're usually only one question away from a better story.

This week's challenge

At the end of a session, ask your player: "What evidence did you collect for your story today?"
