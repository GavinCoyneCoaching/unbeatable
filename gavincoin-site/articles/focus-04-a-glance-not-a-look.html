Focus

A Glance, Not a Look

Here's something that's become genuinely rare: real, sustained attention on one single thing. Most of us — players, parents, coaches, all of us — have got very good at glancing at problems. Skimming past them. We know they're there. We just don't stop and actually look.

A huge amount of problems come down to one of three things: not focusing on the task, not focusing long enough on it, or not focusing correctly on it. It's half measures. A glance, not a look. And I always come back to this — how are you supposed to solve any problem if you don't spend an adequate amount of time actually focused on it? You can't. You're just glancing at it and hoping. When we don't tend to something, it doesn't just sit there quietly. It backs up. One thing you didn't deal with blocks the next thing, which blocks the next — a logjam, then a domino effect — until it all feels like far more than it actually is. But focus a problem properly — really look at it — and something shifts. It's like a picture coming into focus. Suddenly you can actually see it. You can appreciate the depth of it. You can start to see the pathways through it that were there all along, just blurry.

This week's challenge

Pick one thing your player (or you) has been glancing at instead of looking at — a weaker foot, a recurring mistake, an overdue conversation. This week, give it a proper look: "Was that a glance, or was that a look?" Where is my attention right now?
