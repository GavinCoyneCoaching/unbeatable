The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

Not every jar is too full

We spend a lot of time talking about overloaded jars — too many commitments, too much sand. But there's an opposite problem that gets overlooked: the empty jar. Not everyone is overwhelmed. Some people are under-engaged. No goals, no challenge, no direction, no real reason to push forward. At first an empty jar looks attractive — no pressure, no stress, no risk of failure. But over time the lack of challenge becomes its own challenge. Because human beings are built to grow. Sometimes the answer isn't removing more. It's finding something worth adding. I've seen it countless times. A player who once loved the game starts drifting. Training becomes a chore. The spark disappears. Dig deeper and it's rarely a skill or fitness problem. It's a purpose problem. Their jar is empty. And the fix doesn't need to be enormous. Not county selection or national titles — just something that matters. A goal creates direction, direction creates effort, effort creates progress.

This week's challenge

Ask your player honestly: "Is your jar full, or is it empty?" If it's empty, don't add pressure. Help them find one goal — small is fine — that gives the week a reason.
