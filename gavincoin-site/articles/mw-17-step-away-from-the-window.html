Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

Step Away From The Window

We've spent a lot of this series on windows — the opposition, teammates, the referee, the score, past mistakes. So here's the fair question: if not the window, where should a player be looking?

The mirror doesn't ask you to be perfect. It asks you to return. The mirror brings attention back to the only person a player can truly influence — themselves. Their effort, their attitude, their communication, their next decision. And notice what the mirror isn't doing. It isn't asking anyone to ignore the opposition or pretend a mistake didn't happen. It's simply reminding them that none of those things can make the next decision — only they can. The biggest shift I see in players who improve quickly: they stop asking "why did that happen?" and start asking "what can I do next?" Small change in language. Massive change in performance.

This week's challenge

Pick one moment this week to swap that question live, in the moment, right after it happens: "Why did that happen?" → "What can I do next?"
