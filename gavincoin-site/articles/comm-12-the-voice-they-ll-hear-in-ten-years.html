The Voice They'll Hear In Ten Years

We tend to think of our words as one-off moments — said once, landed once, done. But that's not how it actually works with young people. Say something often enough, in enough pressure moments, and it stops being your voice in their head. It becomes theirs. The words they reach for automatically, years later, in a moment that has nothing to do with you at all.

Say it enough times, and it stops being your voice. It becomes theirs. We underestimate the shelf life of our own words. A phrase repeated often enough by a coach or a parent doesn't fade — it becomes part of how that young person talks to themselves, long after they've left your team, your house, your era of coaching them at all. Which makes one question worth sitting with far more useful than most coaching advice: if the people around you repeated your words to themselves every day, would those words help them become who they're capable of becoming?

This week's challenge

Notice the phrase you find yourself repeating most often — to your team, your own kids. If that became their inner voice for good, would you be proud of it? In the final email: a simple way to actually check what your team sounds like — because most of us have never really listened.
