Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

The Chain Nobody Sees

Parents ask me this a lot: why does my child's confidence swing so much, week to week, even when nothing dramatic has happened? Here's what I've come to believe.

Confidence isn't the starting point. It's the last link in a chain. The chain runs like this: better attention → better decisions → better actions → better results → better confidence. Start at the window — attention stuck on a bad call, a stronger opponent, the mistake five minutes ago — and the whole chain suffers. Poor attention leads to a rushed decision, which leads to a poor action, which leads to a worse result, which leads to less confidence next time out. It's not that the player "lost their confidence" out of nowhere. The chain broke at the very first link. Start at the mirror instead — attention on the next job, the next decision — and the same chain runs forward. Better attention, better decision, better action, better result, and confidence follows, because it was earned. That's why we don't chase confidence directly in Unbeatable. We go straight to the first link.

This week's challenge

Next time your player's confidence dips, don't try to talk them back into feeling confident. Ask instead: "Where has your attention actually been?" Fix the first link, and watch what happens to the rest of the chain.
