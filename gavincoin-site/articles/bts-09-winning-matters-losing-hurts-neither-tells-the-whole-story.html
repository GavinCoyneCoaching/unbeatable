Beyond the Scoreboard

Winning Matters. Losing Hurts. Neither Tells The Whole Story.

One of the biggest misconceptions about mindset coaching is that it's all about positive thinking. Smile more, ignore the bad stuff, everything works out. That's not what we do. In fact, one of the worst things we can do for a young player is pretend difficult things aren't difficult. Losing hurts. Getting dropped is disappointing. Watching a teammate take your position is hard. That's real, and pretending otherwise helps no one.

The goal isn't to avoid emotions. It's to learn how to handle them. We can get trapped by those feelings two ways — dismissing them completely, or letting them define us. Neither works. And yes, winning matters. Nobody trains all winter hoping to lose. The problem is when winning becomes the only scoreboard — because then every setback feels permanent. It isn't. The players and athletes we admire most didn't get great by avoiding losing. They got great by learning to respond to it. So inside Unbeatable we keep it to three simple steps: Feel it. Learn from it. Move forward. Feel the disappointment — it's allowed. Learn the one thing it's trying to teach you. Then move forward, because the next action is the only one you can control. And it's only a mistake if you don't learn from it.

This week's challenge

Next time your player has a setback — a loss, a mistake, a tough substitution — walk them through the three steps out loud: What did you feel? What can you learn? What's the next action? In the final email of this series: how a player wins their own scoreboard on the exact day the main one says they lost.
