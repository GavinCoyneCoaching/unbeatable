Frustration vs Fascination

Response-Ability

The referee makes a call that goes against you. A teammate doesn't put the effort in. The weather turns. None of it was your decision, and none of it is going to reverse itself because you're annoyed about it. We've already talked about how much sits outside your window — outside your control. What we haven't talked about yet is what you do have, sitting right in front of you, every time something like this happens.

You don't just have a response. You have a responsibility to develop it. I think of it as response-ability — a duty of care, a requirement on me to react as best I possibly can to the things that happen. I can't control most of what goes on outside my window, on the pitch or off it. But I can absolutely control how I respond and react to it. That doesn't mean pretending everything's fine. You can say "this is a shitty situation, this sucks" — that's fair. But we are where we are. So let's figure it out. That's a far better use of energy than staying frustrated. And here's the honest bit: it's not about things becoming easier. It never really is. It's about us becoming stronger, more focused, better equipped to meet the next challenge — because there's always a next one.

This week's challenge

Next time something goes against you that you didn't choose, ask: "What's actually within my response-ability here?" Not what's fair. Not who's to blame. What's mine to develop.
