The Stairs

Stop Staring At The Top Step

Picture a staircase. Eight, ten, twelve steps up to a goal that really matters. Now picture a player standing at the bottom, staring all the way up at the top. What do they feel? Overwhelmed. Miles away. Not good enough yet.

Nobody climbs a staircase by staring at the top of it. The top step isn't where the climbing happens. The climbing happens on the step right in front of you. When a goal feels impossible, it's almost never because the person isn't capable. It's because they're measuring themselves against the summit instead of the next step. Shrink the focus, and the impossible starts to look like a series of very possible moves.

One step. Then the next. That's the heart of The Stairs. Break the big goal down until the next action is small enough that you can't talk yourself out of it — and then take it. The summit still matters. It's the direction. But your attention belongs one step ahead of your feet. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Take the goal your player owns, and ask one question: "What's the single next step — small enough to do this week?" Not the whole climb. Just the next step. Then let them take it.
