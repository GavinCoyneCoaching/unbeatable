Frustration vs Fascination

Every Moment Is A Crossroads

Stuck in traffic on the way to training. A teammate not trying their best. A kick or a pass you haven't quite mastered yet. None of these are dramatic. None of them make the highlight reel. But every one of them is a decision point. Every day is just crossroads to crossroads. It's easy to think Frustration vs Fascination only shows up in the big moments — the missed penalty, the dropped catch, the disappointing selection. It doesn't. It shows up constantly, in the small stuff, all day, on and off the pitch. And a lot of it doesn't even deserve a reaction. I don't give mental space to everything that happens — if I treated every small thing as a negative, what would that cost me? I don't have to take it on board, store it, replay it. But for the crossroads that do matter, the move is always the same, whether it's a traffic jam or a teammate not pulling their weight: what can I do differently here? What do I need to do, or stop doing, or do more of, do better, do differently, to get a better result out of this? That's the fascination toolkit in five questions. It's the same move, wearing a different outfit, every single time.

This week's challenge

Just for one day, count the crossroads. Not to solve every one — just to notice: "How many small forks did I hit today, and which way did I go?" You'll be surprised how many there are.
