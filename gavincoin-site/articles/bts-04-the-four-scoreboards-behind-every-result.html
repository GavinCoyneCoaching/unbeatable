Beyond the Scoreboard

The Four Scoreboards Behind Every Result

Most of us were taught there are only two outcomes to a match. Win, or lose. Good day, or bad day. Look a little closer and every result is actually one of four very different stories — because how you played matters just as much as what the scoreboard says.

Win or lose is only half the picture. Put the result (did we get the outcome we wanted?) together with the process (did we play the way we train for?) and you get four scoreboards, not two. 1. Win well — right process, right result. The gold standard. You won, and you did it the way you've been training: communicating, working hard, staying disciplined, competing to the end. This is what we're building towards. 2. Win badly — wrong process, right result. The dangerous one. You won, but you got away with it — poor habits, low effort, one or two players carrying the rest. The win hides the problem, which is exactly why winning can be the most dangerous teacher in sport. 3. Lose well — right process, wrong result. The frustrating but healthy one. You competed, communicated and stuck to the plan — and still came out second best. Eight or nine times out of ten, that process wins. There's far more to build on here than most people realise. 4. Lose badly — wrong process, wrong result. The honest wake-up. Everything went against it, and you know why. Painful, but at least it's telling the truth about where you are.

The scoreboard on the wall can't tell these apart. It only ever shows result. It can't see process. So two teams in completely different situations — number 1 and number 2, or number 3 and number 4 — get told the exact same story by the same number on the wall. That's why, inside Unbeatable, we teach players, coaches and parents to read which of the four scoreboards they were actually on. And to be clear — this isn't about caring less about winning. It's about making sure winning means what we think it means.

This week's challenge

After the next match, before anything else, ask: "Which of the four were we today — and why?" Winning badly and losing well are the two everyone gets wrong.
