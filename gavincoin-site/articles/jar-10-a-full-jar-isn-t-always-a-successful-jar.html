The Jar — Why Organisation Matters

A full jar isn't always a successful jar

For years I believed every empty space needed filling. A free hour? Do something. Space in the diary? Book something. Room in the jar? Fill it. That's what productive people do, right? Wrong. Eventually I learned that a full jar isn't always a successful jar — sometimes it's just an overloaded one.

Sometimes the smartest thing to add to your jar is a little room. We talk about empty jars and overflowing jars, about rocks and sand. But there's something just as important: breathing room. Margin. The ability to absorb life when life happens — and life rarely sticks to the plan. When the jar is filled to the brim, every unexpected event becomes a crisis, because there's nowhere for it to go. Here's what surprised me most while building Unbeatable: progress accelerated when I did less, not more. Better energy, better focus, better consistency. It's the same in sport — you don't improve during the session, you improve as you recover from it. The goal isn't to cram in as much as possible. It's to fill your life with the right things and leave enough room to enjoy them.
