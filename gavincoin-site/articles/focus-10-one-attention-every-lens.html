One Attention, Every Lens

If you've read through this whole series — not just this one, but everything we've sent you over the past while — here's something worth pausing on. Every idea we teach really comes back to the same starting point: what's the problem? What's actually going on right now? The lessons aren't separate topics stacked on top of each other. They're the same question, run through different lenses. Here's what I mean, laid out simply: Mirrors & Windows — what are you looking at? What you can't do, or what you can do? The Jar — do you know what's important? Is your jar in order, or overflowing? Are you giving A-level energy to D-level tasks? The Stairs — are you using a ladder, or standing on the stairs? Where are you actually going with this? Beyond the Scoreboard — are you measuring the right things, and enough of them? Or are you living and dying by the wrong metrics? Communication — when you receive a message, are you taking it personally, or can you see it in context — that their issue is with what's going on, not with you as a person? Prioritise Me, Contribute We — are you putting yourself first enough to actually serve the team better?

One attention. Run it through every lens, and you get the whole picture. That's the recap. Not new lessons — the same attention, aimed a little differently depending on what's in front of you. And this isn't just a pitch thing. The same run-through works off the pitch too — a tough week at school, a falling-out with a friend, a decision you're stuck on at home. What's the problem, what are you looking at, what's actually important here, where are you trying to go, what are you measuring yourself against, is this about you or the situation, and are you looking after yourself enough to help the people around you? Same six questions. Different room.

This week's challenge

Next time your player (or you) feels stuck on something, don't try to fix it all at once. Pick one lens from the list above — whichever feels closest — and ask just that one question. "Which one of these actually fits what's going on right now?" Where is my attention right now? In the last email of this series: how it all comes back around to where we started.
