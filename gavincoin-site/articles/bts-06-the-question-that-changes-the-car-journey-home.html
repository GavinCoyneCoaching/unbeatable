Beyond the Scoreboard

The Question That Changes The Car Journey Home

The boot of the car after a match is one of the most important classrooms in sport. It's where kids learn what the adults they love actually value — not from what we say, but from what we ask first. And most of us, without realising it, open with the same line. "Did you win?"

Whatever gets praised gets repeated. Praise goals, and players chase goals. Praise winning, and they chase winning. Praise effort, communication and resilience, and — you guessed it — you get more of all three. The things we praise become the things players chase. It's not a personality trait. It's a habit we build into them, one car journey at a time. So the fix isn't to praise harder. It's to praise better things — and to ask better questions.

How was your CARE score today? Inside Unbeatable we call this checking a player's CARE score — a simple, everyday way to measure the things a player can actually control: C — Communication. Did you talk, organise, lift the players around you? A — Attitude. Did you bring the right attitude, even when it got hard? R — Resilience. Did you bounce back after mistakes and setbacks? E — Effort. Did you give your best to the things you could control? CARE literally means take care of yourself. If a player takes care of their communication, attitude, resilience and effort — that's all they can do. Once those are taken care of, everything else is out of their hands. Unlike the result, all four of those can be measured after every single match, and every single training session. If you want the full version, here's a script worth trying instead of "did you win?": How did you play? Did you enjoy it? Did you try your best? Did you bounce back after mistakes? Did you support your team? Did you listen to your coach? None of those questions need a scoreboard to answer. And here's the funny thing: when players consistently score highly on those hidden scoreboards, the visible one usually takes care of itself. Not overnight, not every weekend — but over time.

This week's challenge

This weekend, retire "Did you win?" for one match. Open with "How was your CARE score today?" instead — and let them do the talking.
