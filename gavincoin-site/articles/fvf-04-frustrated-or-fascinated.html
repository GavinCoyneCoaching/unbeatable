Frustration vs Fascination

Frustrated, Or Fascinated?

Picture a player working on their weaker foot. The first attempts are awkward — the ball goes everywhere, nothing lands where it's meant to. That's not a flaw in the process. That's the process. One of the most important moments in a player's development happens right there, immediately after something goes wrong. Not after the win — after the miss, the bad touch, the awkward attempt. Because that's the crossroads, and the question they ask next decides an awful lot. Frustration ends the conversation. Fascination starts one. Frustration says "this isn't fair." Fascination says "that's interesting." Frustration asks "why is this happening to me?" Fascination asks "what is this trying to teach me?" Back to the weaker foot: the frustrated player stops. The fascinated player keeps experimenting — adjusts the angle, tries again, notices what changes. One sees failure. The other sees feedback. Over time, that gap compounds — not because one player had more talent, but because one stayed curious for longer. Feeling frustrated is completely normal — if you care, you'll sometimes be disappointed. The goal was never to eliminate that feeling. The goal is to stop living there. So the whole philosophy comes down to one simple question, asked right at the fork: Frustrated, or fascinated?

This week's challenge

After the next mistake, ask it out loud — to your player, or to yourself: "Frustrated, or fascinated — which one are we choosing right now?"
