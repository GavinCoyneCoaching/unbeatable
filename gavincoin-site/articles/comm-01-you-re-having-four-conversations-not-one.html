Communication

You're Having Four Conversations, Not One

Think about a normal session. You give the team talk. You pull a player aside mid-match and say something quick and sharp. Later, in the car, you ask your own kid how they got on, and you get "fine" — while their face is saying something else entirely. Three totally different moments. You probably filed them all under the same heading: communication. But they're not the same conversation at all.

You're not having one conversation. You're having four. There's the one from the sideline onto the pitch — coach to player. There's the one between players themselves — teammate to teammate, which is its own minefield, especially in the teenage years. There's the one nobody sees — self to self, the running commentary a player gives themselves in their own head. And there's a fourth one most of us forget about entirely: coach or parent to player, and it isn't about talking at all. It's listening — to the words, and to everything underneath them. Four different conversations, four different sets of rules. Running underneath all of them is the same truth: the message is only successful if it lands. And even when nobody says a word, that's still a message — silence speaks too. Over the next few emails we're going to walk through all four layers, one at a time — what goes wrong in each, and what it looks like to get it right.

This week's challenge

Pick the layer you think about least — for most of us, it's layer four, listening. For one session or one evening at home, just pay attention to it. What did they say — and what did their body say alongside it?
