Focus

One Bill, One Debt, One Conversation

This one's going to zoom out a bit further than usual — on the pitch and off it, because that's what Focus, as the last idea in this series, lets us do. This whole programme actually began with my daughter. Not a drill, not a tactic — a moment where she was struggling with something, and my instinct wasn't to shy away from it or brush it off with "ah, you're being silly" or "sure, it'll be grand." It was: okay, let's focus on it. What could actually happen here? And what's the worst that could happen if it did? And we worked through it, together, properly. That got me looking back over my own life. Nearly forty years of it — twenty in the fitness industry, working in retail as a teenager, all of it. And the times I really couldn't get my head above water, when I was genuinely struggling, it wasn't because life was harder than usual. It was because I wasn't able to focus. Too much on my plate at once, so I couldn't put my action and effort on any one thing. Nothing changed. Nothing got better. Everything got worse — because none of it was actually being tended to, treated or tackled.

One bill. One debt. One conversation. That's how you get your head back above water. When I finally pressed pause and went one item at a time, everything became more manageable. Not because the pile got smaller overnight — because I could finally see the problem, and I could see a pathway through it. Some of those pathways were long. Think about the pathway to winning a championship — that's a season's work, twelve months stacked on top of all the months that came before it. I'll be honest about something else, too. For a while, a lot of my own frustration came from focusing on what other people had — how someone who qualified more recently than me had got further ahead, how someone who, on the surface, hadn't worked as hard as I had ended up with more. That wasn't really a problem with them. It was a problem with my focus. My perspective. That's the honest version of where this whole thing comes from — a dad trying to help his daughter face something, and a coach who'd been there himself.

This week's challenge

Pick the one thing — for you, or for your player — that's been sitting on the pile, unaddressed, adding to the noise. This week, give it one proper block of attention. Just that one thing. "What's the one bill, debt or conversation you've been avoiding — and can you tackle just that one this week?" Where is my attention right now?
