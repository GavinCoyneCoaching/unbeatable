Paying the Price

The Cost Of Comfort

Last time: focus is what turns ability into more ability. But there's something that quietly gets in the way of a player ever applying that focus in the first place. Comfort. Comfort isn't the enemy. Comfort as a default is. In small doses, comfort is fine — necessary, even. After a hard session, comfort is recovery. After a setback, it's how a player resets. The problem starts when comfort becomes the default — the thing a player reaches for instead of the thing they actually need to work on. Think of a player who only ever uses their stronger foot. At first it works. Comfortable, confident, effective. Then opponents figure it out. The weakness gets exposed. And the comfort that used to protect them starts to limit them. The same happens with confidence, with communication, with putting your hand up for responsibility. The things we quietly avoid today have a habit of becoming the exact things that catch us out tomorrow.

So the question worth asking a player isn't "what are you good at?" It's "what are you avoiding?" Not to catch them out — because the honest answer almost always points straight at their next bit of growth. Every player pays a price either way. The temporary discomfort of working on the weak spot, or the long-term frustration of a gap that never closes. One lasts a session. The other can last years. Am I paying the price, or paying the cost?

This week's challenge

Ask your player, gently and without judgement: "What have you been avoiding lately — and what would happen if you leaned into it this week instead?"
