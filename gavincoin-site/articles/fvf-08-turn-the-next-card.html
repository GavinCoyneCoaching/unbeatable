Frustration vs Fascination

Turn The Next Card

Picture a card trick. You pull a card, look at it, slide it back into the deck. I shuffle, split, shuffle again — then start turning cards over. First one — not it. Second — not it. Third, fourth, fifth… After about a dozen, most people watching would assume I'm about to give up. But what if I didn't? Whether your card turns out to be card one or card fifty-one, it's just a case of finding it. That's the fascinated mindset — it rotates. That didn't work — next. That didn't work — next. You don't examine the wrong card. You just turn the next one. Here's the part worth holding onto: it's not that you turn over a wrong card and then stop to pick it apart — "hmm, maybe this one's actually a trick card" — going back and forth over it. No. You just turn the next one. And the next. A player struggles with confidence, tries one thing, it flops, and stops — card one wasn't it, game over. The fascinated player shrugs: interesting, next card. Maybe it wasn't confidence at all — maybe it was preparation, or recovery, or expectations. People quit on card seven. The answer was card eight.

This week's challenge

Name one thing you tried that didn't work. Don't relitigate it — just turn the next card: "That didn't work. What's the next one to try?"
