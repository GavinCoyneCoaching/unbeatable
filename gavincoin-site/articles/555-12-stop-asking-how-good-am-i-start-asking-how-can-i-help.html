Stop Asking "How Good Am I?" Start Asking "How Can I Help?"

If you've followed this series, you've seen the same moment come round a few times in different clothes. The team that collapses without its one star player. The kid who avoids the part of their game they're not good at. The player who holds back not because they're lazy but because they're afraid. The parent giving from a cup that's already empty.

Stop asking "How good am I?" Start asking "How can I help?" That's the shift underneath every email in this series. Not becoming the best player on the pitch — becoming more useful to the people around you. Not carrying the team on your own — finding your way onto the list of people helping to carry it. Not giving until you're empty — filling yourself up so you've genuinely got something to give. Small, consistent contributions from a lot of people will beat extraordinary contributions from a few, almost every time. That's true on a football pitch. It's just as true in a dressing room, a classroom, a house.

This week's challenge

One last question, for you as much as for your player: "Where, this week, could I ask 'how can I help?' instead of 'how good am I?'"
