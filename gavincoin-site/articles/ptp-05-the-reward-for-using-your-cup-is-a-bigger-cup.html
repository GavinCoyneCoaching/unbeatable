Paying the Price

The Reward For Using Your Cup Is A Bigger Cup

Last time: the espresso cup that goes to the brim every trip catches up with the pint glass that only ever gets half-filled. That's the first lesson. Here's the second, and it might matter more.

Your current ability isn't your permanent ability. It's tempting to believe the cup you were handed is the cup you're stuck with. "I'm just not fast." "I'm not one of the better players." Maybe — for now. My youngest plays Gaelic. At six, success was running the right way and passing to her own jersey. Give it ten years and it's tactics, positioning, decision-making under pressure. Nobody handed her a bigger cup. She grew one — through seasons of small, repeated effort.

The reward for effort isn't just the result. It's more ability. Every rep, every mistake survived, every session filled to the brim — it doesn't just get you through today. It expands what you're capable of tomorrow. The cup itself gets bigger. That's why comparison is such a trap. You look at someone else's pint glass and forget they started with an espresso cup too. You see where they are. You don't see the years they spent filling it. Am I paying the price, or paying the cost?

This week's challenge

Instead of measuring your player against someone else's cup, ask them: "Is your cup bigger than it was this time last year?" That's the only comparison that actually tells you anything.
