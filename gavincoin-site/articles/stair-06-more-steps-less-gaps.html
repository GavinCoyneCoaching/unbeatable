The Stairs

More Steps, Less Gaps

When the gap between where a player is and where they want to be feels too large, most people do one of three things: quit, lower the goal, or decide it was never possible. There's a fourth option. Build more steps.

Big goals aren't reached in one leap. They're built from hundreds of small ones, connected together. Almost every challenge becomes easier when we break it down far enough. Not easier as in less effort — easier as in clearer. "I want to become a starter" becomes: improve attendance, fitness, communication, consistency. And "improve fitness" becomes "two extra conditioning sessions each week." That's a real step. Measurable. Climbable. This is where many players get stuck: vague goals, vague plans, vague actions. And vague plans rarely create clear results. It's the same reason confidence is hard to build directly — players treat it as the goal. But confidence isn't a step, it's an outcome. It's what happens once enough small steps have been taken: call for the ball, communicate, try again after a mistake. More steps. Less gaps. Success often has less to do with ambition and more to do with architecture. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Take one vague goal your player has — "get better," "be more confident," "improve" — and turn it into one specific, measurable step for this week. "What does that actually look like — this week, in numbers or actions?"
