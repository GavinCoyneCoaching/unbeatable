Mirrors & Windows — The Power of Perspective

The Moment Teams Start Falling Apart

You've seen it. A bad call. A pass that doesn't arrive. A shot that goes wide. Heads drop, fingers point, communication disappears — and the team isn't playing the opposition anymore. They're fighting themselves.

A window sends attention outward. A mirror brings it back. When pressure rises, players stop focusing on what they can control and start focusing on everything around them — the referee, the crowd, the scoreboard, the mistake from two minutes ago. None of it helps solve the challenge in front of them. The players who look in the mirror first tend to be the most resilient. Not because they make fewer mistakes — because they recover from them faster.

This week's challenge

Pick a sideline cue before this weekend's game — something short you can say the moment heads start to drop. Use it once, and watch what it does.
