Communication

Silence Speaks Too

When people hear "communication," they think talking — instructions, calling for the ball, team talks. But some of the most powerful communication in sport happens when nobody says anything at all. Watch a team concede a couple of early goals. The volume disappears. Heads drop, shoulders slump, the pitch goes quiet. That silence becomes a message all by itself: things aren't going well, and nobody wants responsibility right now. Teammates hear it loud and clear, no words required.

Silence isn't the absence of communication. It's communication. The best leaders I've come across weren't the loudest people in the room. They communicated calm, belief, trust and direction — often without saying much at all — because they understood people are constantly watching and interpreting actions, not just words. Silence speaks too. You're always communicating. Here's the question worth sitting with: if someone watched you for a whole match without hearing a single word, what would they learn about you?

This week's challenge

For one session, pay attention to what your own silence is saying — the folded arms, the dropped head, the sigh. If the sound was off, what story would your body be telling?
