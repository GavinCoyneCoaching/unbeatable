Contribution Isn't Always Visible

A kid comes off the bench for the last ten minutes. Or doesn't get on at all. Walks off feeling like the day didn't count — like sitting there for eighty minutes wasn't really contributing anything. It's an easy trap to measure your worth by minutes played, or by whether your name was in the starting line-up. Contribution isn't always visible. That doesn't make it less valuable. Think of a squad where starters and substitutes have completely different jobs — not different levels of importance, different roles entirely. The player who comes on fresh and changes a game late isn't worth less than the one who started. They're valuable in a different way, at a different moment. That's the reframe worth sitting with: how much more valuable could a player become once they stop measuring their importance by minutes played? The energy they bring in training. The way they lift a teammate who's had a tough week. The standard they hold in the group even when they're not on the pitch. All of it counts. None of it shows up on a scoresheet.

This week's challenge

"What's one way you contributed to the team this week that had nothing to do with minutes played?"
