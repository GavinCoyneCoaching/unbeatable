Communication

Good Intentions, Delivered Poorly

A coach wants a player to improve. That's the intent — good, genuine, nothing wrong with it. But the player walks away feeling criticised, deflated, under pressure. Not because the coach doesn't care. Because the message didn't land the way it was meant to.

Most communication problems don't come from bad intentions. They come from good intentions delivered poorly. That's why one of the most useful questions you can ask yourself, before you speak, is simple: what is my intention here? Am I trying to encourage? Challenge? Correct? Support? Getting clear on the intent first makes you far more thoughtful about the delivery. And here's the part worth remembering long after the conversation ends: people don't remember every word you said. They remember how it made them feel. This doesn't mean lowering the standard — the best communicators are often brutally honest. They're just intentional about it.

This week's challenge

Before your next piece of feedback — to a player, a teammate, your own kid — pause and silently name your intention first. "What am I actually trying to do here — encourage, challenge, correct, or support?"
