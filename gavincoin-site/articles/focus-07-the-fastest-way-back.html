Focus

The Fastest Way Back

If you've followed this series from the beginning, you'll know something by now. Your attention will drift. Mine does. Yours will. Every player's does. The question isn't whether it happens. The question is: how quickly do you come back?

That's the skill. Not staying focused forever. Returning faster. Think about the best players you've watched. It probably looks like they're switched on for the entire match. They're not. They get distracted too. They get frustrated. They make mistakes. They lose concentration. The difference is that they don't stay there. They recover. Quicker. One of the simplest reset tools we teach players is this: Notice. Return. Respond. First — Notice. Become aware that your attention has drifted. Maybe you're thinking about the referee. Maybe you're replaying a mistake. Maybe you're worrying about what happens next. Don't judge it. Just notice it. Then — Return. Bring your attention back to yourself. Back to your role. Back to your effort. Back to your communication. Back to what you can influence. Finally — Respond. Ask one simple question: "What's my next useful action?" That's it. No complicated routines. No long speeches. Just a simple process you can repeat over and over again. Notice. Return. Respond. Every time you do, you're training something far more valuable than confidence. You're training recovery. And in sport, recovery is often what separates consistent performers from everyone else. You won't always get it right. You won't always return immediately. But every time you shorten the gap between distraction and action, you become a more resilient player. Not because you stopped making mistakes. Because you stopped living inside them.

This week's challenge

Pick one moment this week — a mistake, a bad call, a knock to confidence — and walk your player through it out loud: "What did you notice? What did you return to? What was your next useful action?" Don't aim to be the player who never loses focus. Aim to be the one who notices it first and gets back to work the quickest. Where is my attention right now?
