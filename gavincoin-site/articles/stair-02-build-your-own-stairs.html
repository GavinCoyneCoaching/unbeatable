The Stairs

Build Your Own Stairs

Once a player owns their goal, a fair question shows up from the sideline — usually from a coach, sometimes from a parent: "but what about the team?" It's a fair worry. A dressing room full of players chasing purely personal goals doesn't hold together for long. But I don't think the answer is to drop the personal goal. I think it's to build it properly.

Your stairs can be your own — and still belong to something bigger. Here's how it works. The team's staircase is simple: win matches, win the championship. Your personal staircase — better defending, better attacking, sharper kicking or passing, more consistent contribution off the bench, being fitter — isn't a separate climb happening on the side. It's a ladder running up to the team's staircase. The full-back who commits to better one-on-one defending isn't chasing a private project. He's building a step the team goal is actually resting on. The sub who commits to being ready and switched-on the moment the number goes up isn't doing it purely for himself — he's building the team's staircase from wherever he's standing. That's the whole idea in one line: build your own stairs. Own the climb. Just make sure it's connected to something bigger than you. What's my next step?

This week's challenge

Ask your player one question: "What's one personal step you're working on — and how does the team benefit when you take it?" If they can't answer the second half, that's worth a conversation.
