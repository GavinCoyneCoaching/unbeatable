Frustration vs Fascination

What Were You Trying To Do?

A few years ago, at a rugby session, a player tried something ambitious out of position. It didn't come off — the pass went astray, play broke down. An obvious mistake. Two experienced coaches stood nearby. Both saw exactly the same moment. They handled it completely differently.

Great coaches don't remove mistakes. They develop capability. The first coach stepped in straight away: "Don't ever do that again." Understandable — the fastest way to stop a mistake is to remove the behaviour that caused it. The second coach asked something different: "What were you trying to do?" Everything shifted. The conversation moved from the mistake itself to the thinking behind it — timing, support, positioning, decision-making. The mistake wasn't ignored. It became the lesson. One coach protected today's performance. The other invested in tomorrow's player. If we're asking players to be adaptable and step outside their comfort zone, mistakes aren't proof development has failed — often they're proof it's begun.

This week's challenge

Next time a mistake happens in front of you, resist "why did you do that?" and try: "What were you trying to do?" In the final email: what all of this has actually been building towards.
