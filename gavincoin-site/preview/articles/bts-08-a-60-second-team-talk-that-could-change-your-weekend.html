Beyond the Scoreboard

A 60-Second Team Talk That Could Change Your Weekend

We understand all of this perfectly on a Tuesday evening, reading an email like this one. Then Saturday arrives — the nerves, the pressure, the referee — and suddenly it's all about the result again. So here's something practical any coach, captain or player can use immediately.

Before kick-off, ask: "What are our scoreboards today?" Gather the players and walk through five, out loud, before the whistle: Effort — nobody can promise no mistakes. Everyone can promise their best. Communication — will we talk to each other and support each other? Resilience — when something goes wrong, do we drop our heads or respond? Discipline — can we do our own job first, before worrying about anyone else's? Contribution — what's your 5% today, the extra bit only you can add? Finish with one line: "Let's win these scoreboards first, and see where the big scoreboard ends up."

You can lose a match and win every one of these. Or win a match and lose every one. One of those builds long-term success. The other builds long-term problems — even though, on the day, it can feel the opposite way round. It takes sixty seconds. It costs nothing. And it puts the team's attention on the only things they can actually control before a ball is kicked.

This week's challenge

Try the 60-second version before the next match — five scoreboards, one line each, sixty seconds. See what changes, on the pitch and in the dressing room afterwards.
