Direct Doesn't Mean Destructive

Picture a player jogging back after losing the ball. Every coach in that moment has the same instinct — and the responsibility — to address it. The question was never should you say something. It's how. "We need more effort from you." Or: "What are you even doing?"

The problem was never direct communication. It's destructive communication. Those two lines are close in length and close in topic, but they're not close in impact. One focuses on the behaviour. The other attacks the person. One creates improvement. The other creates defensiveness — and usually a player who's switched off for the rest of the half. Unbeatable isn't about lowering standards or removing accountability. Building resilient young people who can handle setbacks and pressure requires honesty — sometimes uncomfortable honesty. But most coaches and parents believe the only alternative to shouting is going soft. There's a third option: intentional communication. Firm, clear, honest — but constructive. Direct doesn't mean destructive, and tough doesn't have to mean toxic.

This week's challenge

Before your next correction, ask yourself one question: "Am I talking about the behaviour — or the person?"
