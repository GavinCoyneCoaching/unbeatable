The Best Teams Don't Just Train Together

Think back to the best team you've ever been part of. Not necessarily the one that won the most — the one where people genuinely got better, where confidence grew, where a mistake turned into a conversation instead of an argument. Chances are, that team had something a lot of others don't.

The best teams don't just train together. They learn together. We tend to assume learning belongs to the coach — he teaches, players listen. But in the strongest teams, learning flows in every direction. Experienced players help younger ones. Players challenge and encourage each other. Nobody's too senior to be taught something by someone else. Here's the catch: most players already know where their missing 5% is. They just avoid working on it, because trying something new in front of everyone is uncomfortable. And fear reduces action. Less action creates less learning. Less learning limits contribution. That's why the best teams deliberately make training the safest place to fail — because matches are expensive places to learn lessons.

This week's challenge

"Who could you help learn something today — and who could you let help you?" Sometimes your biggest contribution isn't scoring the goal. It's giving somebody else the confidence to score theirs.
