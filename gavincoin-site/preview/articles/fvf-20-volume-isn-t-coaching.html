Frustration vs Fascination

Volume Isn't Coaching

Watching my youngest's football, I noticed the pitch was full of instructions — run there, pass now, shoot, turn. Every parent trying to help. Every one of them well-meaning.

Volume isn't coaching. Clarity is. Almost everyone on the sideline thinks they're helping. Parents, coaches, supporters — the intent is good. But good intent doesn't always create good coaching. Sometimes it just creates noise. And sport is already noisy enough — teammates, opponents, pressure, mistakes, emotions, all at once. A young player is processing all of that already. Then we add more and call it coaching. Later in that same game, my daughter picked up a loose ball, made a good run, and played a lovely pass that led to a score. Afterwards, that's all we talked about — not the fifty-eight imperfect minutes, the one where she did exactly what she was learning to do. Because attention is coaching. What we repeatedly highlight becomes what a player believes is important. Shouting "run faster" isn't necessarily coaching. Helping a player see what good looked like is.

This week's challenge

This week, catch and name one good decision out loud — instead of one correction: "That, right there — do that again."
