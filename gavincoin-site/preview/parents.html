For parents

The drive home doesn't need a speech. Just one better thing to say.

You know the forty minutes after a bad result. "Sure, it's only a game" lands as a brush-off; a full post-match review is the last thing they want. It doesn't need dismissing and it doesn't need dissecting — it needs one respectful thing instead.

The moments every parent meets

"Did you win?" — the first question every time. You mean only to connect. But whatever gets asked first gets chased — measure only the result and that becomes all they value.
"You'll be fine, don't worry about it." Reassurance doesn't remove the worry — it just tells them not to have it. So it stays, and now they feel unheard on top of it.
"You were great tonight." When they played poorly and know it, bright praise lands as a lie — and they stop believing the good stuff too.

A better question beats a better speech

Swap reassurance for a question: "What's your job today? What can you control?" Nine times out of ten the kid already has the answer — the nerves were just blocking it.

And a better first question after: not "did you win?" but "how was your CARE today?" — Communication, Attitude, Resilience, Effort. The car ride becomes a classroom instead of a verdict.
